# Reading load: what needs a specialist's eye

For specialists reviewing Thinking Lab lessons for neurodivergent learners (November 2026).
There are 346 lessons. This list picks out the ones where reading is most likely to get in the way, so the review can start there.

## Already done

- Every spoken explanation, worked example and question was checked for sentences over 20 words. 94 were split into shorter sentences, one idea each. Lists were broken up, and some were turned into questions ("Can you afford it now? Will it spoil?").
- Three lines in Grades 4 to 6 were left long, because they have a recorded voice and a new wording would lose the recording. They are in the life cycles, drama and action plan lessons.
- Idioms that could be taken literally were replaced: "red flag" became "warning sign", "learned hands-on" became "learned by doing the work", and "keep you on track" was replaced. "On the other hand" was kept, because the lesson teaches it as a linking phrase.
- A factual error was fixed in the Grade 10 human rights lesson. It said the courts are a Chapter 9 institution, and they are not.

## 2. Lessons with no key-word help

Most lessons have a "What does this mean?" box that explains key words with a picture. 180 lessons have none:

- every Cambridge, IB, IEB and CAPS for SID lesson: 151 lessons
- 29 shared lessons

**Question for review:** this is probably the biggest gap. Do the reviewers agree that these lessons should get word boxes first?

## 4. Questions only a specialist can answer

- **Abstract lessons.** Some lessons are hard because of the ideas, not the wording:
  - Theory of Knowledge (dp11-tok-questions, plus the "TOK link" page at the end of every IB Diploma lesson)
  - the Extended Essay
  - the IB "statement of inquiry" pages
  - literary analysis

  Are these suitable as they are, or do they need another way in?
- **Formulas read aloud.** "x equals negative b, plus or minus the square root of b squared minus four a c" is hard to follow by ear. Should spoken formulas be cut down, so the learner reads them on screen instead?
- **Sensitive topics.** Some lessons describe harm in concrete terms:
  - risky situations (g8-decisions)
  - warning signs of abuse in relationships (g11-relationships)
  - gender-based violence (g10-rights)
  - scams (ad-scams)

  Is the wording right for learners who may take it very literally or find it distressing?
- **CAPS for SID.** Which version of CAPS for SID do the schools use? This affects all 16 SID lessons.
