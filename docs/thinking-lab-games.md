# Thinking Lab games: brief for specialist review

**Status: draft, not yet educationally validated.** These eight games replaced the first Thinking Lab activities on 12 November 2026. They take the place of the "Activity Briefs: Specialist Review Draft" of 1 November, which describes the earlier games.

The Thinking Lab Framework asks the Lab to understand *how* a learner thinks, not whether they reach a right answer. So each game records the learner's process (tests, changes, predictions, how sure they felt) as well as the result. There are two games in each of the four domains.

## What every game has

- **No timer.** Nothing is timed.
- **Up to 3 hints**, from least to most specific.
- **Moving on is allowed.** The learner can move on without an answer at any point, with no penalty.
- **Reflection** at the end. The learner can choose an answer or draw it.
- **Feedback names what the learner did**, for example persistence, a new strategy or careful looking.
- **Two levels**: gentler, and more challenging. Wording changes for older learners.
- **Access**: switch scanning, choosing by resting the pointer, captions, read aloud, and greyscale. Colour is never the only cue: patterns, shapes, line styles and words carry the same meaning. Calm and "No movement" switch off every animation.

## The games

| Game | Domain | What the learner does | What it records |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Light the Village** | Logical and Analytical | Places and tilts mirrors so one beam of sunlight reaches every hut, then shines the light to test | Mirrors placed before the first test (planning) compared with tests needed (trying and adjusting) |
| **The Literal Robot** | Logical and Analytical | Orders instruction cards for Bolt, who does *exactly* what they say. Wrong orders go wrong in funny ways, and the learner debugs | Runs needed, whether they fix one step or start again, extra steps added |
| **The Secret Rule Machine** | Inquiry and Systems | Builds objects (shape, pattern, size) and tests them in a machine to find its hidden rule, then proves it by predicting | Number of tests; **fair tests** (one change at a time); whether a wrong guess sends them back to gather evidence. A wrong guess is answered with evidence from their own tests |
| **Veld Balance** | Inquiry and Systems | Predicts what happens when rain or animals change in a living veld, watches the seasons, then runs their own experiments | Predictions one or two steps away in the food chain; in the experiment, runs and one-change-at-a-time runs |
| **Story Weaver** | Communication and Expression | Builds a picture story (their own character can be the hero), names feelings, and adds their own words by typing, drawing or speaking. It ends as a storybook that reads itself | Preferred way of expressing (choosing, typing, drawing, speaking), feelings named, and for older learners, another character's point of view. No right answer |
| **Describe It** | Communication and Expression | Chooses clues so Bolt can find their creature among look-alikes, then follows Bolt's clues | Clues used compared with the fewest needed (precision), clues that didn't fit, and listening accuracy |
| **Invention Workshop** | Creativity and Metacognition | Designs a way to get water from the river to the garden. Each test shows exactly where and why the water stops. Then meets a new constraint, and names and judges the invention | Tests, revisions after a failed test, different designs tried, and how they judge their own work |
| **How Sure Am I?** | Creativity and Metacognition | Answers puzzles and optical illusions, says how sure they are *before* seeing the answer, and ends with their own "Sure-o-meter" | How well confidence matches being right, and the response when certainty turns out wrong (the illusions) |

## How the levels differ

| Game | Gentler | More challenging |
|---|---|---|
| Light the Village | 4 by 4 village, 1 or 2 huts | 6 by 6, 3 huts, rocks to go around |
| The Literal Robot | Sandwich, going out in the rain (4 steps) | Planting a bean, making tea (5 to 6 steps, with a step that always goes wrong) |
| The Secret Rule Machine | One-feature rules ("It has stripes") | Two-feature rules ("big and has corners", "a star, or it has dots") |
| Veld Balance | Effects one step away | Effects two steps away, for example no cheetahs, so less grass |
| Story Weaver | Who, where, problem, 3 pages | Adds another character's feelings and the lesson of the story |
| Describe It | 4 creatures, 2 rounds of describing, 1 of listening | 6 creatures with more features, and clues said as "NOT" |
| Invention Workshop | Flat ground and a ditch, then "make it flow by itself" | Slopes that need power, then "no wind, 20 coins, no buckets" |
| How Sure Am I? | Counting, a number pattern, odd one out, 2 illusions | 3 illusions, the Fibonacci pattern, a logic puzzle, a subtle odd one out |

## Proposed curriculum links (CAPS; NCF for ages 3 to 4)

| Game | CAPS (proposed) | NCF |
|---|---|---|
| Light the Village | Mathematics: Space and Shape (position and direction). NST: Energy and Change (light travels in straight lines and reflects) | Exploring mathematics; Knowledge and understanding of the world |
| The Literal Robot | Mathematics: Patterns, Functions and Algebra. Technology: algorithms and coding (to confirm) | Communication; Exploring mathematics |
| The Secret Rule Machine | Mathematics: Data Handling (sorting and classifying). Natural Sciences: fair tests | Exploring mathematics; Knowledge and understanding of the world |
| Veld Balance | NST: Life and Living (food chains, ecosystems). Geography: weather and climate | Knowledge and understanding of the world |
| Story Weaver | Home Language: listening, speaking, writing and presenting. Life Skills: feelings | Communication; Creativity; Identity and belonging |
| Describe It | Home Language: listening and speaking. Mathematics: properties of shapes | Communication |
| Invention Workshop | Technology: the design process; Systems and Control. NST: Matter and Materials | Knowledge and understanding of the world; Creativity |
| How Sure Am I? | No direct content area: thinking about thinking. Mathematics: number patterns, comparing lengths | Well-being; Exploring mathematics |

## Questions for specialists

1. Do the "what it records" measures say something meaningful about thinking, and is any of them unfair or open to misreading as a label?
2. Is a "fair test" (one change at a time) a reasonable thing to look for at ages 7 to 12? What about 3 to 6?
3. Optical illusions in How Sure Am I? are meant to show that feeling sure isn't the same as being right. Could this unsettle anxious learners? Should it be optional?
4. Story Weaver lets learners type or speak their own words. Should adults see those words in full, or only how the story was told?
5. Are the two levels right for each age group, and is the wording respectful for learners aged 13 to 25?
